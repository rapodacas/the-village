# Provider Vetting & Scope-of-Practice Policy

*Working name: "The Village" — a private-pay connection service matching San Diego /
California families of children with special needs to independent, non-clinical
special-education-adjacent providers. Not legal or medical advice; confirm with counsel
before launch (see ATTORNEY-BRIEF.md).*

---

## 1. What we are (and are not)

The Village is a **referral registry**. We vet and match independent providers with
families. We are **not** an employer of providers, **not** a law firm, **not** a medical
or therapy provider, and **not** a special-education attorney. Providers are independent
contractors who run their own practices, set their own rates, and serve their own clients.

## 2. Services we connect (non-clinical, private-pay only)

Eligible provider categories:
- IEP / 504 **advocates** (non-attorney)
- **Parent / behavior coaches** (non-medical positive behavior support)
- **Special-needs tutors** and **executive-function coaches**
- **Social-skills group facilitators**
- **Transition / life-skills coaches**
- **Respite providers** — *phase 2 only; see §6 classification note*

**Excluded (do not list):** any licensed clinical or insurance-billable service — ABA,
speech/SLP, occupational therapy, physical therapy, psychology, counseling, or medical
care. These trigger healthcare fee-splitting / anti-kickback law and clinical licensure
we are deliberately staying clear of.

**Private-pay only.** We do not match, bill, or take a fee on any service that is paid or
reimbursed by Regional Center, a school district, Medi-Cal, or insurance. Public dollars
reintroduce anti-kickback exposure.

## 3. Required vetting for every provider (before any match)

1. **Live Scan fingerprinting** — DOJ **and** FBI criminal history.
2. **TrustLine registration** — California's in-home caregiver/tutor registry (checks DOJ,
   FBI, and the Child Abuse Central Index). *Note: for private-pay work TrustLine is not
   legally mandatory, but The Village **requires** it as a condition of listing — it is
   our core trust guarantee and a differentiator vs. informal referrals.*
3. **Identity + right-to-work** verification.
4. **Credential / experience verification** — degrees, certifications (e.g., SEAT for
   advocates), résumé, and **at least two professional references** checked.
5. **Mandated-reporter acknowledgment + training** — every provider (and The Village
   staff) is a mandated reporter under Penal Code §11165.7; annual training and a written
   reporting protocol are required.
6. **Signed provider agreement** including the scope limits (§4), independent-contractor
   terms (§6), and the disclaimers (§7).
7. **Re-screen** background checks on a fixed cycle (e.g., every 2 years) and on any
   credible concern.

No provider is shown to a family until items 1–6 are complete.

## 4. Scope-of-practice boundaries (hard lines)

**Advocates are not attorneys.** They may help parents understand rights, prepare for and
attend IEP/504 meetings, review records, and communicate with schools. They may **not**
give legal advice, represent a family in due-process/litigation, draft legal pleadings, or
hold themselves out as lawyers. Crossing into legal representation is unauthorized practice
of law.

**Coaches and facilitators are not clinicians.** Parent/behavior coaches, EF coaches, and
social-skills facilitators teach skills and strategies. They may **not** diagnose, treat,
or represent their work as therapy, ABA, or medical/psychological care, and must refer out
to licensed professionals when a family needs clinical care.

**Tutors** provide academic instruction only.

Providers must present themselves accurately (title, credentials, what they do and don't
do) in their profile and to families. Misrepresentation is grounds for removal.

## 5. What families are told (disclosure)

Every family sees, and agrees to, a plain-language disclosure that:
- The Village is a **registry**, not the employer of providers and not responsible for the
  services delivered.
- Providers are **independent contractors**; the **family and provider set the rate and
  terms** directly (we suggest a market range).
- Advocacy is **not legal advice/representation**; coaching is **not medical or therapeutic
  treatment**.
- Payments run through **Stripe Connect** directly to the provider; The Village collects
  only its service and membership fees.
- Every provider is **Live Scan + TrustLine screened**, but families make their own hiring
  decision and are encouraged to interview.

## 6. Independent-contractor structure (Labor Code §2777)

We operate as a **referral agency** under **Labor Code §2777** so providers stay independent
contractors (analyzed under *Borello*). To hold that status, and as conditions of listing,
each provider must:
- operate under their **own business name / license** and carry their **own liability
  insurance**;
- **set their own rates** (we publish a suggested range; we do not dictate wages) and be
  free to **negotiate directly** with families;
- **maintain their own clientele** and be free to work elsewhere;
- **accept or decline** any family; control the manner and means of their own work;
- be free from The Village control over how the service is performed.

**⚠️ Respite is different.** Labor Code §2777 excludes "in-home care" from the referral
exemption. In-home respite for special-needs children may fall outside this safe harbor, so
respite is **held for phase 2** pending counsel — it will either be W-2, run under a
different exemption, or omitted. Do not launch respite on the §2777 IC model without a legal
opinion.

## 7. Disclaimers (site + agreements)

- "The Village is a referral registry. We do not employ providers and are not responsible
  for the services they deliver."
- "Advocates are not attorneys and do not provide legal advice or representation."
- "Coaches, tutors, and facilitators do not provide medical, psychological, or therapeutic
  treatment and do not diagnose."
- "The Village does not provide special-education, legal, medical, or therapeutic services
  itself."

## 8. Founder firewall (conflict of interest)

The founder is an employed public-school special-education teacher. To manage conflict-of-
interest exposure (Gov. Code §1126, Political Reform Act §87100, district incompatible-
activities policy — see ATTORNEY-BRIEF.md):
- **No referrals involving the founder's own school district** (and ideally not neighboring
  districts the founder's district interacts with).
- The founder **recuses** from any district decision touching a family or provider connected
  through The Village.
- The business is operated **on personal time and resources only**; no district equipment,
  student data, or work time is used.
- Confirm compliance against the founder's **specific district outside-employment /
  statement-of-incompatible-activities policy** before launch.
